Where you are

Coldstream Canyon

Lost Trail sits on the creek in Coldstream Canyon, on the quiet side of Donner Lake just outside Truckee. Cold Stream Road runs up-canyon toward the Sierra Crest and the Pacific Crest Trail; downhill it drops you into Truckee and onto I-80 in a few minutes. You're genuinely off-grid, but not far from a great coffee, a resort chair, or a trailhead.

May–October

Summer & fall

Trailheads out the door, granite up the road, and cold water in every direction. A sampler — ask us and we'll match it to your group.

  • Hike from the door

    The Coldstream Trail climbs up-canyon to meet the Pacific Crest Trail near Tinker Knob and Mt. Judah — big-view ridgeline days that start at the lodge.

  • Mountain biking

    World-class Truckee–Tahoe singletrack: the Donner Lake Rim Trail, Emigrant Trail, and the flowy Tahoe National Forest network. Shuttle or pedal.

  • Gravel & road

    Quiet forest roads up the canyon and classic road loops around Donner Lake and up to Donner Summit for the climbers among you.

  • Rock climbing

    Donner Summit is a Sierra granite classic — sport, trad and top-rope walls 15–20 minutes up the old highway. Big Chief near Truckee for bolted granite.

  • Swim & paddle

    Donner Lake for a morning swim or SUP; Lake Tahoe's north shore beaches a bit farther. The creek at the lodge for a cold plunge after a hot day.

  • Fly fishing

    The Truckee River is a blue-ribbon wild-trout fishery. Little Truckee, Donner and alpine lakes round it out — we can line up a guide.

  • Trail running

    From creekside cruisers to the PCT high above the canyon. The Donner Lake Rim Trail is the local benchmark.

  • Fall color & quiet

    September–October is the sleeper season: aspen turning, empty trails, warm days and cold, clear nights on the deck.

November–April

Winter & spring

Some of the deepest, most reliable snow in the Sierra — with cat skiing and touring straight from the canyon and a wall of resorts a short drive away.

  • Private cat skiing

    Our partner Pacific Crest Snowcats runs guided snowcat skiing across roughly 3,000 acres of exclusive Sierra terrain — arranged with your stay.

  • Backcountry touring

    Skin from the canyon toward Tinker Knob and the crest, or tour the classic Donner Summit lines. Bring a guide if the group is new to it.

  • Downhill resorts

    Sugar Bowl on Donner Summit is closest (~15–20 min); Palisades Tahoe, Northstar, Boreal and Donner Ski Ranch are all inside ~30 minutes.

  • Nordic & skate

    Royal Gorge — the largest cross-country resort in North America — plus Tahoe Donner XC, both a short drive for groomed kilometers.

  • Snowshoe & fat bike

    Quiet canyon and forest routes right from the lodge — the mellow way to get out between bigger days.

  • Snowmobile

    Sled access is part of the winter approach and a fun add-on; we can arrange rentals and guided riding.

  • The ski-in arrival

    Winter access is an over-snow trip by ski, snowshoe or sled from the plowed parking below — a real backcountry arrival, and part of the magic.

  • Know before you go

    This is avalanche terrain. Check the Sierra Avalanche Center daily, carry beacon-shovel-probe, and hire a guide if you're unsure. We'll share current conditions.

Truckee

In town

Historic downtown Truckee — Commercial Row and the surrounding blocks — has everything you'll need, 15–25 minutes down the canyon.

Provisions & fuel

Full grocery runs at Save Mart and Safeway; New Moon Natural Foods for organic and specialty. In summer, the Truckee Community Farmers Market. Fuel up in Truckee before you head up the canyon — there's no gas past town. Stock the lodge on the way in; you're off-grid once you arrive.

Gear & rentals

Truckee is a mountain-sports town: Tahoe Mountain Sports, Truckee Sports Exchange and Paco's Truckee Bike & Ski for rentals, tuning, backcountry gear and local trail beta. Renting bikes, skis, snowshoes or touring kit for the group is easy — tell us and we'll help set it up ahead of time.

Eat & drink

Food & drink

Prefer to stay in? We'll line up a private chef. Heading to town, here are the local staples we send people to.

  • Coffee & breakfast

    Coffeebar for the morning cortado; Squeeze In and Wild Cherries for a proper breakfast; Truckee Bagel Company to fuel a trail day.

  • Lunch & casual

    Full Belly Deli for sandwiches, Burger Me for the group, and Best Pies or a slice when everyone's hungry and undecided.

  • Dinner out

    Moody's Bistro in the historic Truckee Hotel, Pianeta for Italian, Cottonwood above town for the view — and Trokay when it's a special night.

  • Beer & a nightcap

    FiftyFifty Brewing and Alibi Ale Works for local pints; Bar of America on Commercial Row for a cocktail and live music.

A starting list, not gospel. Truckee's food scene moves fast and reservations help on weekends — text us before your trip and we'll share current favorites, book ahead, or arrange a private chef at the lodge.

Worth the drive

Day trips & detours

When you want to roam a little farther than the canyon.

  • North Lake Tahoe

    Tahoe City and Kings Beach are ~25–35 minutes out — beaches, boat rentals, lakeside lunch, and the CA-89 river run back toward home.

  • Donner Summit & history

    Donner Memorial State Park, the old Highway 40 overlook, the historic railroad snowsheds and China Wall — a stunning, story-rich half-day.

  • Reno & the Riverwalk

    ~50 minutes east: the closest airport, plus a walkable riverfront, museums and a night out if the group wants city lights.

  • Hot springs & soaks

    End a big day with a soak. Ask us about current options and access in the greater Tahoe–Sierra area — some are seasonal or require a short walk.

Before you come

Good to know

A few honest notes so the trip runs smooth. Full arrival directions, access and an emergency card come with your booking.

Elevation & weather

You're around 6,000 feet — drink extra water the first day and expect big day-to-night temperature swings, even in summer. Mountain weather turns quickly; pack layers and a rain shell any month.

Getting in

The canyon road is unpaved — AWD or 4×4 recommended in summer, and required-plus-over-snow-travel in winter. Fuel and provision in Truckee; there's nothing past town.

Connectivity & power

Cell service is spotty in the canyon, but the lodge has Starlink wifi and off-grid solar power with generator backup. Download maps offline before you leave town.

Bears & leave-no-trace

This is black-bear country: keep food inside and secured, and pack out what you pack in. Leave the canyon better than you found it — it's why it's still this good.

In an emergency, call 911. The nearest hospital is Tahoe Forest Hospital in Truckee. For winter conditions check the Sierra Avalanche Center; for roads, Caltrans QuickMap / 511. Your booking packet includes the lodge's exact address, access details and an on-site emergency contact.
